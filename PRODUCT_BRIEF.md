Fly Design Engine — Product Brief

Product Vision

Fly Design Engine is an AI-assisted fly design, construction, education, visualization, and modification platform.

It is not primarily a fly recipe database or material organizer.

The central product experience is:

Design it. Build it. See it. Understand it. Modify it.

The application teaches users to understand why a fly is designed and constructed a particular way, rather than simply telling them what materials to use.

⸻

The Problem

Current fly-tying education is fragmented.

Information is commonly organized around individual patterns, videos, articles, or material lists.

This creates several problems:

* Materials lists may be incomplete or inconsistent.
* Tutorials often assume foundational knowledge.
* Patterns are not consistently organized by species, forage, mechanics, or intended behavior.
* The relationship between a material and its function is often unexplained.
* Component positioning and proportions are frequently left to the tyer’s interpretation.
* Tutorials generally show how to tie a fly without explaining why it is designed that way.
* There is little connection between fly construction and predicted performance.
* Modifying an established pattern often requires the tyer to understand the underlying design principles independently.

The Fly Design Engine should address these gaps.

⸻

The Core Opportunity

The application should move fly tying education from:

“Here’s a recipe. Copy it.”

toward:

“Here’s what you want the fly to do. Let’s design it.”

The application should function as a comprehensive center for both new and experienced fly tyers.

Beginners should be able to learn foundational concepts.

Experienced tyers should be able to explore design decisions, mechanics, materials, proportions, and alternatives.

⸻

The Product’s Central Concept

A fly is a physical system composed of spatially related functional components.

The system therefore needs to understand more than:

Hook + material A + material B + material C.

It needs to understand:

* What is being used
* Why it is being used
* Where it is positioned
* How much is used
* How it is constructed
* How it relates to other components
* What mechanical characteristics it contributes
* What the completed fly should look like
* What the fly is intended to accomplish

⸻

Choose Your Own Adventure

The defining interaction should feel like a choose-your-own-adventure for fly design.

The user should be able to begin with a goal rather than a pattern.

For example:

“I want a four-inch streamer for brown trout that gets deep quickly but glides when I pause it.”

The application should guide the user through meaningful decisions.

The user chooses among design objectives and sees the consequences of those choices.

The application should explain:

* What each decision accomplishes
* What it costs
* What it changes
* What alternatives exist

The user remains the designer.

The AI is the guide, teacher, and design assistant.

Foundational Education

A major weakness in current fly-tying instruction is that foundational concepts are often assumed rather than taught.

The application should eventually provide foundational learning around:

* Hook anatomy
* Hook sizing
* Thread control
* Thread tension
* Material control
* Material quantity
* Tie-in points
* Proportion
* Profile
* Weight placement
* Densities
* Water absorption
* Material movement
* Water displacement
* Drag
* Buoyancy
* Sink rate
* Hook orientation
* Construction sequence
* Finishing techniques

The user should learn principles that transfer between patterns.

Species records should eventually connect:

For example:

Sculpin

↓
Compact profile

↓

↓

↓
Fly design implications

The system should use forage information to inform architecture, mechanics, materials, and proportions.

⸻

Mechanics-Centered Design

The application should allow users to design around desired characteristics.

Potential characteristics include:

* Movement
* Sink rate
* Buoyancy
* Profile
* Drag
* Water displacement
* Stability
* Translucency
* Flash
* Flexibility
* Water absorption
* Durability
* Hook orientation

The application should explain interactions between characteristics.

For example:

Increasing weight may increase sink rate, but its position can also affect orientation and movement.

The system should identify conflicts and tradeoffs.

⸻

Material Intelligence

Materials should not simply be catalogued.

The application should understand their functional contributions.

For example:

Material
↓
Physical properties
↓
Functional contribution
↓
Mechanical effect
↓
Design suitability

A material recommendation should answer:

Why this material?

It should also answer:

What else could I use?

and:

What changes if I substitute something else?

⸻

Position Is Fundamental

The system must recognize that where something is placed can matter as much as what it is made from.

A material placed at the rear of a fly may have a different functional contribution than the same material placed near the head.

Therefore component position must be represented as structured design information.

⸻

Proportion Is Fundamental

The application should teach relative proportion rather than relying solely on absolute measurements.

Examples:

* Tail length relative to hook shank
* Wing length relative to body
* Head size relative to hook gap
* Body width relative to hook gap
* Material density relative to desired profile

This allows designs to scale across hook sizes.

⸻

Construction Is More Than a Recipe

Construction instructions should communicate:

What

What component is being added.

Where

Where it is positioned.

How

How it is attached.

How Much

The quantity or density required.

Why

What purpose it serves.

What It Changes

Its expected mechanical contribution.

This turns a tying tutorial into an educational construction system.

Examples:

* Glide

The MVP should use conceptual visualization rather than pretending to perform exact hydrodynamic simulation.

The user should be able to ask:

Why is it this long?

⸻

AI’s Role

AI should not simply be a chatbot sitting beside a fly recipe database.

AI should act as:

* Instructor
* Design guide
* Natural-language interface
* Explainer
* Comparison engine
* Design modification assistant

However:

AI should not be the authoritative source of structured design state.

The application’s structured models should remain authoritative.

AI interprets language and explains system decisions.

⸻

Design Modification

The application should allow users to modify designs using natural language.

Examples:

Make it sink faster.

Give it more movement.

Make the profile narrower.

Move the weight back.

Make the head smaller.

The application should translate these requests into structured design changes.

The consequences should then propagate through:

* Mechanics
* Materials
* Position
* Proportions
* Construction
* Visualization
* Behavioral predictions
* Explanation

⸻

Original vs. Modified

The application should preserve design history.

A user should be able to compare:

ORIGINAL

with

MODIFIED

The comparison should show:

* What changed
* Why it changed
* Expected mechanical effects
* Benefits
* Costs
* Tradeoffs
* Visual differences
* Construction differences

This makes experimentation part of the learning experience.

3. Species selection
4. Forage selection

6. Desired mechanics
7. Architecture recommendation
8. Functional components
9. Material assignment
10. Component positioning
11. Proportion

13. Visual fly representation
14. Behavioral prediction

The MVP should favor depth over breadth.

⸻

Second Benchmark

After the Brown Trout → Sculpin experience works, the next major benchmark should be:

4-INCH GLIDE STREAMER

This is important because it tests whether the system can design around desired mechanics rather than simply reproducing an established pattern.

The system should be able to reason about:

* Size
* Movement
* Sink rate
* Glide
* Profile
* Weight
* Materials
* Component placement
* Construction
* Tradeoffs

If it can do this successfully, the core design-engine concept is beginning to work.

⸻

What This Product Is NOT

The application must not become primarily:

* A fly recipe database
* A material catalog
* A collection of YouTube tutorials
* A pattern search engine
* An AI chatbot with fly-themed graphics

Those functions may exist, but they are secondary.

The core product is a fly design and learning engine.

⸻

Product North Star

The application should allow a fly tyer to say:

“Here’s what I want this fly to do.”

and ultimately receive:

Here’s what to build.

Here’s where everything goes.

Here’s how to build it.

Here’s why it’s designed that way.

Here’s what it should look like.

Here’s what we expect it to do.

Here’s what happens if you change it.

The ultimate goal is not simply to help people tie more flies.

It is to help people learn how to design flies.
